Steam Machine starts at US$ 1,049: what Valve confirmed and what is still unclear

Valve's living-room PC has an SSD and memory the owner can swap, but the price rose because of RAM. Here are the specs and what we don't know about Brazil.

By Aloha TeamNovember 7, 2026, 14:18

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

The Steam Machine, Valve's compact PC built to sit next to the TV, left the drawing board in June. On the 22nd, Valve opened a reservation list on Steam with price and specs, and sales began on June 29, according to Wikipedia and Omelete. I read four pages to write this piece (Omelete, Terabyte, Wikipedia and Notebookcheck). The latest ones go up to August, and I found no information on stock or deliveries after that, so treat what follows as a snapshot of the launch.

How much it costs

The 512 GB model sells for US$ 1,049 and the 2 TB one for US$ 1,349 (Wikipedia's text says US$ 1,348, a one-dollar gap between the table and the body of the page). With the new second-generation Steam Controller in the bundle, prices rise to US$ 1,128 and US$ 1,428. In Europe, the 512 GB model costs €1,039.

In reais, Omelete mentions about R$ 5.2 thousand for the base model and R$ 7.2 thousand for the 2 TB with controller. These are conversions made by the article itself, without tax or shipping, not an official price. The same text says the machine will not be sold officially in Brazil. It is the only one of the four sources to say so, and it does not cite Valve. Terabyte, published a day later, talks about a “pre-sale” and a price in reais without saying where it can be bought. Since the two accounts don't match, check Valve's store before planning any purchase.

Valve itself admitted, according to Eurogamer as cited by Wikipedia, that the price came out “significantly” above what it had imagined. In February, the company had already warned it would review price and schedule because of rising RAM costs. On July 17, Bloomberg heard Valve engineers say they don't expect memory prices to fall before the end of the year.

What's inside

The spec sheet, which Wikipedia compiles from Eurogamer and IGN, is that of a mid-range PC. The processor is a custom AMD Zen 4 with 6 cores and 12 threads, up to 4.8 GHz. The graphics card is an AMD RDNA 3 with 28 compute units and 8 GB of GDDR6 memory. There is 16 GB of DDR5 RAM and the system is SteamOS, based on Linux. Wikipedia compares the package to a Ryzen 5 7540U and a Radeon RX 7600M, a comparison that is its own and not Valve's.

To connect to a TV there are DisplayPort 1.4 and HDMI 2.0. Wi-Fi is 6E. The unit weighs 2.6 kg, measures about 16 centimeters on each side and has an LED bar on the front, behind a removable cover. There is a detail to check here: Wikipedia's table lists one USB-C port, but the text of the same page mentions two.

What you can swap

In February, Valve confirmed on its blog, as Notebookcheck cites, that the M.2 2280 SSD and the DDR5 memory modules can be replaced after purchase. Wikipedia recalls that the company once talked about two 8 GB sticks and later clarified that the factory model has a single 16 GB one. In short, you can increase storage and memory without a repair shop.

4K, but with an asterisk

At first, Valve talked about games in 4K at 60 frames per second with FSR and ray tracing. On June 26, it changed the phrase to “up to 4K with FSR 4.1”, according to IGN as cited by Wikipedia. The change matters: FSR is AMD's technology that rebuilds the image from a lower resolution, and the new wording makes clear that 4K depends on it.

For games, Valve inherited the Steam Deck verification program: whatever is already “Verified” on the handheld counts as verified on the Steam Machine, with a target of 1080p at 30 frames per second. Journalists cited by Wikipedia compare performance to the PS5 and the Xbox Series S. If you want to install Windows, you can: Valve released drivers in July, but dual boot, with both systems side by side, is not supported.

Waitlist, scalpers and what's missing

Valve sold through a randomized reservation queue and admitted the initial quantity was “less than we wanted to be able to make”. According to IGN, on June 27 reservations were already being resold on eBay for more than double the price. Two pieces of the family announced in November 2025 were left out of the four pages I read: the Steam Frame, a virtual reality headset, has no price or date in the sources, and the second-generation Steam Controller only appears as a bundle accessory, with no standalone price.

Sources: Omelete (in Portuguese), Terabyte (in Portuguese), Wikipedia and Notebookcheck (in German).
