Witch on the Holy Night, ufotable's film, opens in Brazilian theaters on January 28

Japan release on November 20; in Brazil, theaters only and with subtitles. What is known about the adaptation of Kinoko Nasu's visual novel.

By Aloha TeamNovember 9, 2026, 19:23

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

In Brazil, Witch on the Holy Night, the animated film ufotable has adapted from a 2012 visual novel, opens in theaters on January 28, 2027. Crunchyroll announced the date, according to Omelete, and Anime Corner confirmed it in its country-by-country release list. That is the Brazilian date, since each country has its own. In Japan, the film opens earlier, on November 20, 2026.

Theaters only, with subtitles

According to Omelete, the Brazilian release is theatrical only, with the original Japanese audio and Portuguese subtitles. Distribution will be handled by Crunchyroll and Sony Pictures Entertainment. The article does not mention a dub. Nor do the pages I read give a ticket-sale date, a theater chain or the running time, so it is worth following the exhibitors' announcements closer to the date.

Brazil is part of a large wave. Anime Corner lists January 28 for Mexico, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Italy, Portugal, Australia and other countries as well. Germany comes two days earlier, on January 26, and France on January 27. The United States, Canada and the United Kingdom get January 29, and Spain February 5. In every case these are the dates reported when the release was announced, and they may change. Because each country has its own date, readers outside Brazil should check the one for their own country.

Where the story comes from

The source is Mahoutsukai no Yoru ("Witch on the Holy Night" in English), a visual novel created by TYPE-MOON and written by Kinoko Nasu, released in Japan on April 12, 2012, according to Anime Corner. The film has had a long wait: it was announced in December 2021, the first teaser came out in December 2023, and the November 20, 2026 date appeared only in a trailer this July, according to Anime Corner. Two lists of November premieres, from Legit.ng and Duniaku, give the same Japanese date. The story is set in the late 1980s and follows Aoko Aozaki, a high school student who is an apprentice mage, according to Omelete.

Who is in the film

The animation is by ufotable, the studio behind Demon Slayer (Kimetsu no Yaiba), as Espinof points out. Final Weapon, in its article on the October trailer, names Hikaru Kondou as chief director, Masaru Takeuchi as director, Moe Tsuzuki on character design and Hideyuki Fukasawa on music. For the voice cast, the same site lists Haruka Tomatsu as Aoko Aozaki, Yūsuke Kobayashi as Soujyuro Shizuki and Kana Hanazawa as Alice Kuonji. The theme song is "Yoru to Ao" by supercell. All of these names and the song title come from a single source, so they should be treated as information to be confirmed.

Can you get to know the story beforehand?

Yes. Anime Corner says the English version of the game came out in December 2022, and Final Weapon reports releases on Nintendo Switch and PlayStation 4 on December 8, 2022, and on Steam on December 14, 2023. The two pages differ only in the platforms they mention, not in the dates. What none of the sources says is whether the film is a self-contained story, whether you need to have played the game, or whether a sequel is planned. They call it "a film," singular, and that is all we can state.

When ufotable or Crunchyroll release the running time, tickets and theaters, we will update this article. The official trailer, from a Sony Pictures channel and carrying the January 28 date, is below.
