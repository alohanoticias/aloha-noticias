The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping opens November 19 in Brazil: cast, runtime and what is still unknown

Pre-sales open since October 15, 158 minutes of film and Haymitch at the center of the story. Here is what is already confirmed before you buy a ticket.

By João GabrielNovember 5, 2026, 21:18

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, official teaser trailer with Portuguese subtitles (Paris Filmes channel)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

On November 19, the film The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping arrives in Brazilian theaters, going back to Panem decades before Katniss. Ticket pre-sales started on October 15 and there are preview screenings on November 18. The date in the United States is different: November 20.

What the story is about

The film adapts Suzanne Collins's book, published in Brazil by Rocco, and follows Haymitch Abernathy, Katniss's future mentor, back when he himself was picked for the Games. According to NC News, this edition is special: instead of two young people per district, there are four. Joseph Zada plays Haymitch, and Ralph Fiennes plays President Snow.

It is directed by Francis Lawrence, who has already led other films in the series. The film is distributed in Brazil by Paris Filmes.

Cast

Besides Zada and Fiennes, the cast lists released include Whitney Peak (Lenore Dove), Mckenna Grace (Maysilee Donner), Ben Wang, Elle Fanning (Effie), Jesse Plemons (Plutarch), Glenn Close, Kelvin Harrison Jr. (Beetee), Maya Hawke, Billy Porter and Kieran Culkin as host Caesar Flickerman. The lists from the sources are not identical: some names appear in one and are missing in others, so the final check is the film's official credits.

Two articles, Gameover Drive and Escutai, say that Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson take part in the film, based on an interview the actress gave to the Call Her Daddy podcast in late October. They differ on the exact day (October 27 or 28) and do not explain how the two appear. Treat it as press information, with no official detail.

The setting: the 50th Games

According to Rolling Stone Brasil, the story takes place at the 50th edition of the Games, called the Second Quarter Quell, with 48 tributes, double the usual number, roughly 25 years before Katniss's story. It is the edition in which Haymitch, a tribute from District 12, comes out victorious. NC News also mentions four young people per district, so the math checks out.

On Rolling Stone Brasil's list, Elle Fanning plays Effie Trinket, Jesse Plemons is Plutarch Heavensbee, Kelvin Harrison Jr. plays Beetee, Maya Hawke is Wiress, Lili Taylor is Mags Flanagan and Whitney Peak plays Lenore Dove Baird, Haymitch's girlfriend. Mckenna Grace is Maysilee Donner, a District 12 tribute who was the former owner of the mockingjay pin. Ralph Fiennes plays an older Snow; the young Snow of the previous film, *The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes*, also directed by Francis Lawrence, was played by Tom Blyth. Haymitch was played by Woody Harrelson in the original films.

Runtime and first reactions

The film runs 158 minutes, or 2h38, according to Escutai and Cinema de Buteco, the latter citing Omelete. You can plan your screening: it is the longest in the franchise, according to Cinema de Buteco.

The first reactions came out after a screening in London on October 28. What Cinema de Buteco reports is mostly positive, with praise for Zada, but it includes a more restrained opinion: a familiar story for those who know the series, with a third act that works. First reactions tend to be more enthusiastic than final reviews, so it is worth waiting for the critics.

Another set of reactions comes from Einerd, which describes the online reception as “overwhelmingly favorable.” Among the reactions it cites, Gavin Spoors wrote that it is “almost a perfect Hunger Games film, and more brutal than you imagine,” and Matt Neglia said Joseph Zada “impresses greatly in an extremely demanding role.” According to the same text, some fans already place it above *The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes*, and readers of the book say the ending does not disappoint.

What is not confirmed yet

None of the pages I read gives the age rating in Brazil, and there is no confirmation of which screenings will be dubbed or subtitled. You can find that on the Paris Filmes session page or on your local theater's website. On streaming, there was no announced date as of November 2.

One detail for people comparing articles: some count this as the fifth film in the series, others as the sixth, depending on how you count. Here we treat it as the franchise's new adaptation, without numbering it.
