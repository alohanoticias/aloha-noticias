Why consoles and PCs got more expensive in 2026: the memory crisis explained

Artificial intelligence data centers are buying much of the world's memory output. That has already pushed up PlayStation 5, Switch 2 and Steam Machine prices, and the pressure continues into year-end.

By Aloha TeamNovember 22, 2026, 10:48

AI-fueled memory chip shortage could raise prices of everyday tech (CBS News channel)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

Anyone who checked prices for a console, laptop or graphics card in 2026 noticed: almost everything went up. The reason manufacturers themselves cite most is the same, memory. DRAM chips, which become devices' RAM, and NAND chips, used for storage, have become much more expensive.

What happened

The short explanation is artificial intelligence. Data centers running AI services need huge amounts of memory, and the few companies that make these chips have shifted production toward servers, where margins are higher. That leaves less for PCs, phones and consoles.

Research firm TrendForce, which tracks this market, recorded sharp increases throughout the year. In the second quarter, it projected conventional DRAM contract prices rising 58% to 63% over the previous quarter.

Consoles felt it

Sony announced a worldwide PlayStation 5 price increase on March 27, effective April 2. In the United States, the disc model went from $549.99 to $649.99, and the PS5 Pro from $749.99 to $899.99. The company cited global economic pressures; trade press pointed to memory as a central factor.

Nintendo was more direct. When it announced in May the Switch 2 increase, from $449.99 to $499.99 in the US on September 1, president Shuntaro Furukawa named the memory shortage as the main reason. According to broadcaster WPXI, he also said the new price does not cover all of the extra cost.

And PCs?

On PC, the bill shows up in both individual parts and prebuilt machines. Valve, for example, launched the Steam Machine from $1,049 and had warned in February that it would revise price and timing because of rising memory costs, as Omelete notes.

For people who build or upgrade their own computers, that means RAM sticks and SSDs cost more than a year ago. Graphics cards are affected too, since graphics memory follows the same trend.

Will it get better?

Not soon. For the last quarter of 2026, TrendForce still projects conventional DRAM contract prices up 10% to 15% and NAND up 15% to 20%. The pace has slowed, but prices keep rising, and the firm says memory supply for PCs could shrink in 2027 as more capacity goes to servers.

There's a sign of a ceiling: TrendForce has been saying that consumer customers, such as PC and phone makers, are close to the most they can pay. Even so, new factories take years to come online, so relief depends on supply that doesn't exist yet.

What to do in practice

For anyone thinking of buying a console or PC, the lesson of 2026 is that waiting did not guarantee a lower price. Compare calmly, watch year-end deals and check that the cheaper model has enough memory and storage, because upgrading later has also become expensive.
