Blade Runner 2099 arrives on Prime Video on November 25 with all 8 episodes at once

The series is set 50 years after Blade Runner 2049, stars Michelle Yeoh and Hunter Schafer, and, according to three sources, tells one complete story.

By João GabrielNovember 15, 2026, 13:48

Blade Runner 2099, NYCC trailer (Prime Video channel)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

Binge mode is on: all eight episodes of Blade Runner 2099 land on Prime Video at once on Wednesday, November 25. Amazon itself and four Brazilian outlets (Game Over Drive, Einerd, Critical Hits and Omelete) agree on the date and the format. There is no weekly rollout, so no waiting for the next episode. Dates below are for Brazil; other countries may differ.

The show is exclusive to the service. According to Amazon it premieres in more than 240 countries and territories, and in Brazil, as Game Over Drive points out, you need an active Prime Video subscription. The pages I read do not give the release time in Brazil, so I cannot say whether episodes appear overnight or in the morning.

Where the story starts

The plot jumps half a century. The series takes place in 2099, 50 years after the events of *Blade Runner 2049*, the 2017 film directed by Denis Villeneuve. The official synopsis, quoted by Omelete, places the action in Los Angeles, in a city where replicants, the franchise's artificial humans, now run things and humans have become second-class citizens. The sources disagree on how that happened: Critical Hits speaks of a replicant uprising, Game Over Drive of a war between replicants and humans.

That shifts the axis of the story. Instead of starting with someone hunting replicants hidden among people, the starting point is a world where the other side is in charge. That reading is mine, based on the synopsis: the sources do not describe the plot beyond this, and none of them spoils anything.

Who is in front of and behind the camera

Hunter Schafer plays Cora, a human on the run. Michelle Yeoh plays Olwen, a replicant who is also a Blade Runner and is in the last days of her life, according to Amazon. Dimitri Abold and Lewis Gribben are listed as main cast by Amazon and Game Over Drive, but Einerd and Omelete treat them as recurring. The recurring cast includes Katelyn Rose Downey, Daniel Rigby, Johnny Harris, Amy Lennox, Sheila Atim, Matthew Needham, Tom Burke and Maurizio Lombardi.

The creator and showrunner is Silka Luisa, of *Shining Girls* (*Iluminadas* in Brazil). Ridley Scott, who directed the 1982 Blade Runner, is an executive producer, and Jonathan van Tulleken directs the first two episodes. The production comes from Amazon MGM Studios, Alcon Television Group and Scott Free Productions, according to Game Over Drive.

Is it a closed story or will there be a second season?

Here the sources almost agree. Amazon calls it a "limited series". Einerd says the showrunner confirmed, in an interview with Collider at Comic-Con, that the story is a single one, with all eight scripts finished before shooting and no second season. Omelete also speaks of only one season. The exception is Game Over Drive, which calls what is premiering the "first season" without saying the story ends. I go with the majority reading, and Einerd's is the most detailed.

Do I need to watch the films first?

No source says it is mandatory. Since the series is set 50 years after *2049*, and Einerd and Omelete treat it as a continuation of that film, anyone who has seen at least *2049* should catch more references. Game Over Drive adds that Rick Deckard and K, the protagonists of the films, are not expected to appear and that the series revisits concepts from Philip K. Dick's book. That comes from a single source, so treat it as likely, not certain.

The trailer has also caused some date confusion. Game Over Drive and Critical Hits say it appeared at San Diego Comic-Con on July 24; Amazon says it was revealed at a New York Comic-Con panel on October 10. There may have been more than one release (the Prime Video channel has a trailer and a teaser), but the pages do not explain this. Critical Hits also finds the trailer sunnier and less gloomy than the films, an impression from a single outlet.

What is still open: I did not find in the sources the release time in Brazil, the age rating or any word on dubbing at launch. When Prime Video publishes them, check the series page before planning your marathon.
