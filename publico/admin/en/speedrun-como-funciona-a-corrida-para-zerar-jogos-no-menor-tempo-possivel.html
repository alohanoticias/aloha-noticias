Speedrunning: how the race to finish games as fast as possible works

Categories, rules, timers and charity marathons that raise millions. Here's what's behind world records and where Brazil fits into the story.

By Aloha TeamNovember 28, 2026, 09:32

Hollow Knight: Silksong in 54 minutes at AGDQ 2026 (Games Done Quick)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

Finishing a 30-hour game in 20 minutes sounds impossible, until you watch a speedrun. The goal is to reach the end credits in the shortest possible time, following rules set by the community itself. What started as a pastime on internet forums became a hobby with leaderboards, records contested by hundredths of a second and marathons that raise millions of dollars for charity.

Categories: not every record is the same

Each game has several categories, and that is what makes comparisons fair. The most famous is any%, in which anything goes to reach the end, regardless of how much of the game was completed. In the 100% category, the player must collect or complete everything the game requires. There are also glitchless versions, which ban the use of programming bugs, and specific categories created by each title's fans.

Super Mario 64 is a good example. Records are split by the number of stars collected: 120, 70, 16, 1 and even 0 stars. Each category requires different routes and techniques, and some only exist because players found ways to skip parts the game would, in theory, make mandatory.

How a record is validated

Most leaderboards live on speedrun.com, which hosts rankings for tens of thousands of games. To get on the board, the player submits the full video of the run, with the timer visible, and volunteer moderators check whether the rules were followed. Time is usually measured with programs like LiveSplit, which splits the run into segments and shows, in real time, whether the player is ahead of or behind their best result.

There is also an important difference between RTA and TAS. RTA, short for real-time attack, is a run done by a person, controller in hand. TAS, short for tool-assisted speedrun, is a run programmed frame by frame in an emulator. TAS runs show a game's theoretical limit and don't compete with human records.

The marathons that became charity events

The world's biggest event is Games Done Quick, created in 2010 in the United States. Twice a year, runners take turns live for a whole week, streamed online with donations from viewers. The January 2026 edition, Awesome Games Done Quick, raised about $2.44 million for the Prevent Cancer Foundation. Across all editions, the event has passed $60 million donated, according to the organizers.

The format has its problems too. In 2022, a runner was banned from the event for presenting a pre-recorded run as if it were live, which shows why the community takes verification so seriously.

And Brazil?

Brazil has a strong community that is recognized abroad. The main national marathon is Brazilians Against Time, or BRAT, created in 2016 by speedrunner Hugo "Hugo4fun" Carvalho after he took part in Games Done Quick. The first edition was held in person in São Paulo and benefited AACD, a Brazilian rehabilitation charity. Since then, BRAT has held online and in-person marathons with Brazilian runners and donations to charities.

How to start

You don't have to be a record holder to speedrun. The advice from the community is to pick a game you really love, because there will be hundreds of attempts. Then just look up its page on speedrun.com, read the category rules, watch the best times and install a timer. The first goal isn't the world record, but your own personal best.
