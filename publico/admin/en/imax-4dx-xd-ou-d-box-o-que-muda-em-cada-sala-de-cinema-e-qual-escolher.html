IMAX, 4DX, XD or D-BOX: what changes in each movie theater and which to choose

Giant screens, louder sound, moving seats, wind and water in your face. With the big year-end releases coming, here is what each format really offers.

By Aloha TeamNovember 14, 2026, 18:36

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

When you buy a ticket, the app shows an alphabet soup: IMAX, 4DX, XD, D-BOX, Macro XE. They all cost more than a regular screen, but they don't deliver the same thing. Some focus on picture and sound; others on motion and physical effects. Knowing the difference helps you spend wisely, especially with the big December releases.

IMAX: picture and sound first

IMAX was created by Canadian company IMAX Corporation in the 1960s and came into use the following decade, according to Brazilian site Canaltech. The idea is to offer higher resolution and more immersive sound, with speakers spread around the room. Diário do Litoral notes that IMAX is not just a bigger screen: it is a package involving filming, projection and the design of the room itself.

That is why the experience varies. Some films are shot with IMAX cameras and change aspect ratio in certain scenes, filling more of the screen's height. Diário do Grande ABC, for example, reports that Dune: Part Three, opening December 17, will have IMAX screenings. It is worth checking whether a film was made with the format in mind.

XD and Macro XE: the chains' big rooms

XD (Extreme Digital) is Cinemark's own format. According to Olhar Digital, the screen is about 40% larger than a regular one and the sound seven times more powerful. Screenings can be in 2D or 3D. Cinépolis has an equivalent format, Macro XE, also with a larger screen and bigger sound than the chain's standard rooms.

4DX and D-BOX: movies that move you

4DX was developed in 2009 by South Korea's CJ CGV, according to Canaltech. Seats sit in blocks on platforms that tilt, shake and vibrate in sync with the film, as Hardware.com.br described. There are also environmental effects such as wind, water and smells, according to Olhar Digital.

D-BOX goes the same way but only with seat motion, without the environmental effects. In Brazil it is exclusive to Cinemark, according to Canaltech. Each seat has an individual control to raise the intensity or turn it off, plus a weight sensor, according to TecMundo. Programming each film takes 300 to 400 hours of studio work, Folha Vitória reported when the seats arrived in the state of Espírito Santo.

Which to choose

It depends on the film and on you. For productions with lavish visuals, huge landscapes and a strong score, IMAX or XD are usually worth more. For action, racing or adventure films, 4DX or D-BOX can be fun, but anyone who gets motion sick easily or wants to focus on dialogue may prefer to sit still. One last tip: before paying extra, check the theater's website to see which technology that room really has, because names vary from chain to chain.

Sources: Canaltech, Canaltech, salas premium, Olhar Digital, Diário do Litoral, TecMundo, Folha Vitória, Hardware.com.br and Diário do Grande ABC.
