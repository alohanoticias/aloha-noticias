2.4 GHz or 5 GHz Wi-Fi: which to use for your console and TV, and when a cable still wins

The 5 GHz band is faster and suffers less interference, but loses strength through walls. Here is how to choose and how to force the switch on a console.

By Aloha TeamNovember 20, 2026, 10:34

TecMundo Explains: Wi-Fi, the difference between 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz (TecMundo channel, in Portuguese)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

Many routers create two networks: one at 2.4 GHz and another at 5 GHz, sometimes with similar names, sometimes with the same name. For browsing on your phone, the difference barely shows. But for downloading games, playing online or watching 4K movies on the TV, picking the right network can change the experience a lot.

Speed, range and interference

The general rule is simple: 5 GHz is faster but reaches less far; 2.4 GHz goes farther but is slower. Brazilian site Tecnoblog cites Intel figures: up to about 100 Mb/s at 2.4 GHz, up to 1 Gb/s at 5 GHz and up to 2 Gb/s on the 6 GHz band found in newer routers. These are theoretical maximums; in practice it depends on the device, the distance and your internet plan.

Range follows wave physics: lower frequencies pass through walls and furniture better. That is why the 2.4 GHz signal usually reaches the back bedroom more strongly, while the 5 GHz signal drops faster when there are obstacles in the way.

Interference, on the other hand, works against 2.4 GHz. TecMundo compares channels to lanes on a road: that band has only three non-overlapping channels, against more than 20 on the 5 GHz band. In buildings full of routers, 2.4 GHz turns into a traffic jam. Canaltech notes that microwaves and Bluetooth devices also use that band and can get in the way.

Which network to use for each device

For consoles, TVs and computers, the most common advice is 5 GHz, as long as the device is fairly close to the router. Canaltech lists the PS5 and Xbox Series among the devices that benefit from it. Phones and laptops do too, when they are in the same room or the next one.

The 2.4 GHz network makes more sense for devices far from the router or that need little speed, such as smart bulbs, plugs and cameras. Game Overdrive adds a useful caveat: if the console sits in the farthest room, behind several walls, the 2.4 GHz connection may be more stable, even if slower.

An important warning: neither band fixes high ping when the problem lies with the provider, the route to the server or the game's server itself. Switching networks improves the part that happens inside your home, not the rest of the journey.

When a cable is still the best option

If you can run a network cable to the console or TV, it is still the most stable choice. Game Overdrive cites the recommendation from router maker TP-Link: for gaming, Ethernet first; if that is not possible, 5 GHz or 6 GHz Wi-Fi.

When the router uses the same name for both networks, the device chooses on its own, and not always well. On the PS5, you can force the band: go to Settings, then Network, Settings and Set Up Internet Connection; wait for the scan to finish, press the Options button and choose 5 GHz under Wi-Fi Frequency Bands, as Tech Advisor explains. On other devices, the option may be in the router's app.
