Five games made in Brazil that won over players abroad, and where to play each one today

From a frantic FPS that passed 1.8 million players to a racer pulled from stores in June, the list shows how far Brazilian studios have come.

By João GabrielNovember 23, 2026, 13:08

Mullet MadJack, launch trailer (Epopeia Games)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

Brazil still has no game the size of a GTA, and maybe it doesn't need one. What the country has are small studios that, with lean teams, landed games on best-of-the-year lists, on Game Pass and on Nintendo Switch. I picked five that show different paths, with what each achieved and how to play it today. Prices are from the Brazilian Steam store on November 12, 2026, and may change with sales.

Mullet MadJack (Hammer95, 2024)

It is the most recent phenomenon. Developed by Hammer95 and published by fellow Brazilian company Epopeia Games, it is a first-person shooter with the look of 1980s and 1990s anime, in which the hero has to kill an enemy every ten seconds to stay alive. Released on Steam on May 15, 2024, it passed 1.8 million players across Steam and Xbox, won six awards between the NYX Game Awards and SBGames, and reached Nintendo Switch in April 2026.

Where to play: PC (Steam and GOG), Xbox and Nintendo Switch. On Steam in Brazil it was R$ 32.46.

Dandara (Long Hat House, 2018)

Created by a duo of computer scientists from Belo Horizonte, Dandara flipped the platformer rulebook: the heroine doesn't walk or jump, she leaps from surface to surface, ceilings included. The character is inspired by Dandara dos Palmares, who fought slavery in 17th-century Brazil. The game came out in February 2018 and later got the Trials of Fear edition, with new content.

Where to play: PC, consoles and mobile. On Steam in Brazil, the Trials of Fear Edition was R$ 46.99.

Unsighted (Studio Pixel Punk, 2021)

An action RPG in which every character, the heroine included, has a lifespan that really ticks down while you play. The idea of constant pressure won critics over: the game made several 2021 best-of lists in foreign outlets, such as Kotaku Japan's.

Where to play: PC and consoles. On Steam in Brazil, R$ 49.99.

Kaze and the Wild Masks (PixelHive, 2021)

For anyone who grew up with 16-bit platformers, Kaze is almost a love letter: a rabbit girl who wears masks with animal powers, pixel-art levels and a pace that recalls the genre's classics. It came out in March 2021.

Where to play: PC and consoles. On Steam in Brazil, R$ 37.99.

Horizon Chase Turbo (Aquiris, 2018), the one that left the stores

It is the best-known case and, today, the saddest. Porto Alegre studio Aquiris turned nostalgia for racers like Top Gear into a franchise that, according to the company, passed 80 million downloads across all versions. Aquiris was bought by Epic Games, and in March 2026, after Epic laid off more than a thousand employees, came the announcement: Horizon Chase and Horizon Chase Turbo stopped being sold on June 1, 2026.

Anyone who already bought it can still download the game. For everyone else, the way in is Horizon Chase 2, which remains on sale.

What these five have in common

None relies on expensive licenses or cutting-edge graphics. All of them bet on a clear idea, easy to explain in one sentence, and a visual identity you can recognize from afar. There is also a lesson in Horizon Chase's story: even a hit can vanish from stores when the company that owns it changes course. If any of these caught your eye, it is worth getting it while it is available.
