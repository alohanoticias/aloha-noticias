Switch 2 reaches 26.35 million, but half-year sales fall 37%: what Nintendo's report shows

The console sold 6.49 million units from April to September, versus 10.36 million a year earlier. Software, on the other hand, sold more, and the annual target was kept.

By Aloha TeamNovember 19, 2026, 14:23

Nintendo Switch 2 – First-look trailer (Nintendo of America channel)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

Nintendo released its results for the first half of its fiscal year, April to September 2026, on November 5. The headline cuts both ways: Switch 2 has now sold 26.35 million units since its June 2025 launch, but the pace has slowed a lot compared with its first year.

The console numbers

In the half, Switch 2 sold 6.49 million units, down 37.4% from 10.36 million in the same period of 2025. By quarter, that was 3.82 million from April to June and about 2.67 million from July to September, versus 4.54 million in the same quarter a year earlier.

An important detail: Nintendo counts as sales what it ships to stores and distributors, plus direct sales on its websites. It is not the number of consoles that reached players' hands.

Even with the drop, Switch 2 remains ahead of the original Switch and the PlayStation 5 at the same point in their lives, by more than 6 million units, according to Video Games Chronicle. The site notes that both rivals were supply constrained in their early years.

Software sold more

On the software side, the trend went the other way. Switch 2 games sold 24.49 million units in the half, up 18.8%, and the lifetime total reached 73.21 million.

Among the period's releases, Splatoon Raiders sold 2.42 million, Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave 1.34 million and Star Fox 1.17 million. Mario Kart World and Pokémon Pokopia, from the previous year, kept selling well.

What about Switch 1?

The original Switch sold 1.27 million consoles in the half, down 32.7%, reaching 157.20 million in total. Its games, which also run on Switch 2, rose 6.8% to 65.73 million. The standout was Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream, with 8.81 million units.

Price was part of it

The drop is not only about comparing with the launch year. In May, Nintendo announced price increases due to rising memory costs, driven by demand from artificial intelligence data centers. In Japan, Switch 2 went from 49,980 to 59,980 yen on May 25. In the United States it went from $449.99 to $499.99 on September 1, and in Europe from €469.99 to €499.99.

According to US broadcaster WPXI, company president Shuntaro Furukawa named the memory shortage as the main reason for the increase and said the new price does not cover all of the extra cost.

Lower revenue, target kept

Consolidated net sales fell 12.1% to 966 billion yen. Even so, Nintendo kept the forecasts it made in May for the fiscal year ending March 2027: 16.5 million Switch 2 units and 60 million games. To hit the target it is counting on the holidays, with The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, released on November 5, the Switch 2 version of Pikmin 4 and Xenoblade Chronicles 3 – Nintendo Switch 2 Edition on December 3.

The takeaway

Switch 2 is selling less than in its launch year, but owners are buying more games. The bridge to Switch 1, which runs older games and has more than 157 million units worldwide, remains an advantage Nintendo cites in its own report. The next results, covering Christmas, come out in February and will show whether the price increase weighed on the holidays.
