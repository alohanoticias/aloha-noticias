GameCube and Xbox turn 25: the November 2001 week that changed consoles

In four days, the United States got Microsoft's first console and Nintendo's first to use discs. One sold 24 million; the other, 21.74 million. Both left a legacy that lasts to this day.

By João GabrielNovember 22, 2026, 19:09

Nintendo GameCube - Nintendo Classics – Nintendo Direct | Nintendo Switch 2 (Nintendo of America channel)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

November 2001 had one of the busiest weeks in video game history. On the 15th, Microsoft launched the Xbox in the United States. On the 18th came Nintendo's GameCube, already on sale in Japan since September 14. This month, both turn 25 in the US market.

Neither won that generation. The PlayStation 2, released a year earlier, passed 155 million units and remains the best-selling console ever. But each left marks that are still visible today.

Microsoft's newcomer

The Xbox hit US stores at $299.99, with Halo: Combat Evolved among its launch games. That was the game that gave the console its identity, according to The National, in a lineup that also included Project Gotham Racing and Dead or Alive 3.

Inside, it was almost a PC: it had a built-in hard drive and an Ethernet port, uncommon for a console back then. Microsoft had already shown off the machine with Bill Gates on stage alongside wrestler and actor Dwayne Johnson, The Rock.

The console sold about 24 million units, 16 million of them in North America. In Japan, where it arrived in February 2002, it never took off. But the audience it built carried over to the Xbox 360, and Halo became one of gaming's best-known series, with Halo 2 popularizing Xbox Live.

Nintendo's cube

The GameCube was Nintendo's first home console to drop cartridges. It used optical discs smaller than a regular DVD, 8 centimeters wide, and had a handle on the back. At the US launch, the highlight was Luigi's Mansion.

Commercially it underperformed: it sold 21.74 million units. Among Nintendo's home consoles, only the Wii U sold fewer. Even so, it got games like Super Smash Bros. Melee, Metroid Prime, The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker and Super Mario Sunshine.

There was even a Japan-only version, the Panasonic Q, which also played DVDs. And the GameCube controller became so beloved that Nintendo rereleased it years later to promote Super Smash Bros. Ultimate on Switch.

And today?

The GameCube came back in another form. Since June 5, 2025, the Nintendo GameCube – Nintendo Classics library has been part of Nintendo Switch Online + Expansion Pack, on Switch 2 only. It started with F-Zero GX, The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker and Soulcalibur II and has added games like Luigi's Mansion and Super Mario Sunshine.

Nintendo also sells a new version of the GameCube controller for Switch 2, wireless, with a USB charging cable, but only to Nintendo Switch Online members and for use with that classics collection.

The week that stuck

In four days, the market gained a new company willing to lose money to get into the game, and saw Nintendo test a format that carried on with the Wii and Wii U. Twenty-five years later, Microsoft is still in the business, and games from that purple cube are on sale again.
