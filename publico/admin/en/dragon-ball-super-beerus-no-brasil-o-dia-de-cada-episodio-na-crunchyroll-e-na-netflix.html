Dragon Ball Super: Beerus in Brazil, the Day Each Episode Streams on Crunchyroll and Netflix

Toei's anime has been on air since October 11, with one episode a week. Here is what each platform says, HBO Max included, and what remains unanswered.

By Aloha TeamNovember 10, 2026, 14:06

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

If you missed the start of Dragon Ball Super: Beerus, there is still time to catch up: the anime premiered on October 11 and releases one episode a week. The catch is that it is on more than one service, each with its own day of the week. This guide covers Brazil: the days and platforms below are the Brazilian ones, and availability and release days in other countries may differ. Here is what the sources say.

Crunchyroll: Sundays

Crunchyroll started first. It premiered on Sunday, October 11, and episodes keep coming on Sundays, according to Omelete (which published the announcement on October 5) and Ei Nerd. Ei Nerd reports that episode 3, on October 25, came out at 1 p.m. Brasília time. If the schedule really is weekly, simple math puts the fifth episode on November 8. That is our own estimate, not an official calendar.

Netflix: Saturdays, about a week later

Netflix confirmed the anime for Brazil on October 2, with a catalog premiere on October 17 and weekly episodes, according to Omelete. Omelete also says the one-week delay behind Crunchyroll repeats what happened with Dragon Ball Daima. Ei Nerd gives October 31 for episode 3 on Netflix, which matches a weekly count from the 17th. But Ei Nerd itself notes that dates may change.

HBO Max: Fridays

This is the platform people talk about least. Only Critical Hits reports that HBO Max premiered the anime on October 16, a Friday, with new episodes always on Fridays. The site also writes that the audio languages available at launch were not confirmed and recommends checking the title's page within the service. Since this comes from a single source, check your own catalog before counting on it.

What this version is

According to Omelete, it is an enhanced version of the Beerus arc, told with more attention to his point of view: the God of Destruction wakes up after years, hears about a Saiyan who defeated Frieza and goes after Goku. The production is by Toei Animation, and Akira Toriyama is credited for the original work, according to Critical Hits. That same site is careful to say it is not a continuation of the end of Dragon Ball Super and should not be confused with The Galactic Patrol, a separate production.

Descriptions of the changes vary from article to article. Omelete mentions redone scenes and a reworked narrative, Ei Nerd cites visual improvements, editing adjustments and added scenes, and Critical Hits speaks of renewed animation. No source lists the differences in detail. They also disagree on how far the story goes: one Omelete article mentions the Resurrection of Frieza and the Universe 6 tournament, while another talks about Resurrection 'F', with Frieza's return and the debut of Super Saiyan Blue. The exact limits remain an open question.

What nobody has said

None of the pages I read confirms dubbing or subtitles on each platform, and none says how many episodes the anime will have. Premiere times on Netflix and HBO Max do not appear either. The safest route is to open the title's page on the service you subscribe to and check the audio and subtitles there. If Toei or the platforms release the episode count and the language list, we will update this article.

Sources: Omelete (Netflix), Omelete (Crunchyroll), Omelete (full guide), Critical Hits and Ei Nerd (all in Portuguese).
