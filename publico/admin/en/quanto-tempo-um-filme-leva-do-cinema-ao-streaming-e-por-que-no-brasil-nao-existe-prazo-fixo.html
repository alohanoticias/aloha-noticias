How long a movie takes to go from theaters to streaming, and why Brazil has no fixed deadline

I'm Still Here took 21 weeks to reach Globoplay. Other films show up at home in little more than a month. The difference lies in what the industry calls release windows.

By João GabrielNovember 25, 2026, 09:52

I'm Still Here opened in Brazilian theaters on November 7, 2024, and only reached Globoplay on April 6, 2025, after 21 weeks on screens. Other films make the same trip in less than two months. Anyone waiting to watch a new release at home tends to assume there is a rule behind that timing. There isn't, at least not in Brazil. What exists is a commercial arrangement with a technical name: the release window.

What is a release window?

It is the period during which a film is available exclusively on one kind of screen before moving on to the next. The classic order starts in theaters, then goes to digital rental and purchase, then subscription streaming, pay TV and, last, free-to-air TV. Each step is a new chance to sell the same film, and exclusivity is what gives each one its value. If a new release were in a platform's catalog on opening day, a good part of the audience would skip the theater.

One detail confuses many people: digital rental and subscription streaming are different windows. A film can go on sale in digital stores weeks before it joins the catalog of a platform you already pay for. When a search shows the title with a price, it is not yet "on streaming" in the sense of being included in your monthly fee.

From 90 days to 17, and back to 45

Until 2019, Hollywood's benchmark was to keep a film in theaters only for about 90 days. It wasn't law, it was custom, backed by pressure from theater chains. The pandemic broke that model. In July 2020, Universal struck a deal with the American chain AMC that let it release films for digital rental after just 17 days, the equivalent of three weekends. In 2021, Warner went as far as opening all its films in theaters and on HBO Max at the same time in the United States, and dropped the strategy the following year.

Since then, the movement has been in reverse. In April 2026, at CinemaCon, Paramount chief David Ellison promised an exclusive 45-day theatrical window. Universal guaranteed five weekends of exclusivity for its remaining 2026 releases and seven starting in 2027. American exhibitors, grouped in the Cinema United association, defend 45 days as the minimum for the whole market.

And in Brazil, what does the law say?

Today, nothing. The gap between the theater and the platform is set by contract between the distributor, the theaters and the streaming service, case by case. That may change with bill PL 8.889/2017, known as the Streaming Bill, approved by the Chamber of Deputies in November 2025 by 330 votes to 118. The text bans putting a film released in theaters on a video-on-demand platform before nine weeks, about 63 days, counted from its premiere.

The number used to be higher. An earlier version of the proposal set 180 days, a period that the rapporteur on the Chamber's Culture Committee, congresswoman Jandira Feghali, considered excessive and cut. Platforms want the window removed from the text, and the exhibition sector wants to keep it. As of publication, the bill was awaiting a floor vote in the Senate, with no date set.

Why do some films arrive quickly and others take a while?

Three factors weigh the most. The first is who owns the film. Studios with their own platform, like Disney, take their releases to their own service, and the timing is an internal decision. I'm Still Here was a Globoplay original production, so its destination was set from the start. The second is the box office: a film that keeps selling tickets stays on screens longer, and one that flopped tends to reach digital rental sooner, to recover revenue while people are still talking about it. The third is licensing contracts, which can take a title to a different platform than many people expected.

How to find out when a film will arrive at home

The safest path is to follow the official accounts of the distributor and the platforms, which announce the digital release date and the catalog entry. Aggregators such as JustWatch show where each title is available in Brazil and separate subscription, rental and purchase. Keep in mind too that when a film arrives on streaming it usually leaves theaters: if you want to see it on the big screen, the time is while it is among the releases of the week.
