Godzilla Minus Zero: do you need to see the first film? Questions and answers before you buy a ticket

The sequel opened November 5 in Brazil and continues straight from Minus One. We answer what you can know before heading to the theater.

By Aloha TeamNovember 9, 2026, 14:21

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

Godzilla Minus Zero opened in Brazilian theaters on November 5. Since it is the continuation of an Oscar-winning film, many people are asking whether they can go in without preparation. Straight to the questions.

Do I need to see the first film?

No source I consulted says it is mandatory. But the sequel is direct: according to Omelete, the story is set in 1949, two years after the events of *Godzilla Minus One*, and follows the Shikishima family again. Our recommendation, then, is to watch the first one beforehand if you have time. You get more out of the characters and understand better what changed.

Where can I watch the first one?

The 2023 film won the Oscar for Best Visual Effects. It had a theatrical re-release in October in Brazil, according to Einerd, and streaming availability changes over time, so there is no point naming a platform here. Look the title up on a streaming search tool such as JustWatch before you watch.

To get a sense of the first film's scale: *Godzilla Minus One* cost about US$15 million and grossed about US$113 million worldwide, according to Ingresso.com.

What is known about the plot?

The official synopsis says Godzilla is more powerful and has New York as a target, according to Omelete. Critical Hits says the trailer confirms Ghidorah as the sequel's villain. Beyond that, the sources I read do not detail the story, and that is for the best.

Is IMAX worth the extra cost?

This is a case where the question makes sense. According to Omelete, it will be the first Japanese production shot for the IMAX format, and Einerd notes special IMAX screenings on November 3, before the general release. It is a large-scale film, so the bigger screen counts. But check your theater's page to see which rooms have IMAX, because not every city does.

When does it open, and what about showtimes?

The general release was November 5 in Brazil, according to Ingresso.com. Showtimes and whether the film is dubbed or subtitled depend on each chain, and the sources themselves warn that the schedule may vary by city.

Who directs, and who is back?

Takashi Yamazaki directs and co-writes the film, according to Ingresso.com, and Girônerd adds that he also supervised the visual effects. Both sources confirm Ryunosuke Kamiki back as Shikishima, with Minami Hamabe also in the cast.

How long is it?

Ingresso.com reports 135 minutes (2 hours 15), which would make it the second-longest film in the franchise, behind the 1998 American *Godzilla* at 139 minutes. Girônerd also cites 135 minutes, based on the New York Film Festival schedule, but lists 2 hours 05 on the film's info card, so confirm the running time on your theater's page before booking dinner afterward.

What have the first reactions been?

The world premiere was at the 2026 New York Film Festival, according to Ingresso.com. Girônerd says the first reviews were positive, with 96% approval on Rotten Tomatoes across 23 reviews, praising the larger scale and Yamazaki's direction. It is a small sample that changes over time, so treat the number as a snapshot.

Is it worth it?

It is too early to say, since the sources I read have no final reviews. If you liked the first one, the trailer gives good reasons to go. For people who have not seen it, it is a good chance to rewatch the previous film before the screening.

Sources (in Portuguese): Omelete, Einerd, Ingresso.com and Critical Hits. Also consulted: Ingresso.com (running time) and Girônerd (both in Portuguese).
