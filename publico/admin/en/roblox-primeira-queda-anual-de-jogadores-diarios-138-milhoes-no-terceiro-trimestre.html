Roblox posts its first annual drop in daily players: 138 million, 9% fewer than in 2025

Third-quarter results show fewer people playing but higher revenue. The causes are still guesses, and the company does not point to a single one.

By João GabrielNovember 14, 2026, 13:59

Roblox Sees AI Content Surge as Daily Active Users Fall (Bloomberg Tech)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

On October 29, Roblox released its third-quarter numbers, and one of them stole the show: 138 million daily active users, 9% fewer than in the same period of 2025. According to GGRecon, it is the first annual drop in the company's history. Gaming.net, which reproduces the earnings report, confirms the figure and the change.

What fell and what rose

The quarter is a mixed picture. Daily active users grew 12% on the previous quarter but stayed below the level of a year earlier. Hours played came to almost 34 billion, 15% less than in 2025 and 17% more than last quarter. Game Developer notes that the 138 million also fell short of the 145 million analysts had expected.

The money tells a similar story. Bookings, which measure what players spent during the period, were $1.697 billion, 12% lower than a year ago but above the top of Roblox's own guidance. Accounting revenue reached $1.572 billion, up 16%. The gap comes from accounting rules: GGRecon explains that the company recognizes past purchases as revenue over time, so the two lines can move in opposite directions. Monthly unique payers fell 16%, to 30.3 million, according to WN Hub.

What may explain the drop

Roblox does not blame the result on a single cause. GGRecon points to the age checks, which began to apply in 2026, and to restricted chat for accounts that have not passed them. By the end of the quarter, 64% of daily players had completed the check (about 75% in the United States and the United Kingdom, per the same site). Gaming.net's numbers show who is on the other side: users aged 18 and over make up 29% of age-checked users worldwide, and 37% in the United States.

GGRecon also says that over-18s are the fastest-growing group, with daily players up 18%, and that retention is the highest since 2021. Both claims appear in that source only, so treat them with care until the company gives more detail. How much of the drop actually comes from age checks is something none of the pieces I read can say.

The bet on artificial intelligence

In the same report, the company talked a lot about AI. According to Game Developer, more than 80% of the quarter's top 100 games were made with AI tools, and CEO David Baszucki described a step change in how fast content can be created. For players, that could mean more releases every week. Whether it brings people back, the report does not answer.

What Roblox expects for the end of the year

The fourth-quarter outlook is not an immediate rebound. The company expects bookings between $1.89 billion and $2 billion, which would be 10% to 15% below the same quarter of 2025, and revenue between $1.47 billion and $1.58 billion, 4% to 12% higher. GGRecon reports that shares fell about 16% the day after the release, a figure I could not confirm in a second source.

For players in Brazil, nothing changes right away: the age and chat rules have applied there since June, and that is where the debate over the drop will continue. The main question is still open: whether 138 million is a floor or the start of a trend, and only the next earnings reports will say.
