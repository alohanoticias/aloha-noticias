The most common scams targeting gamers, and how to avoid every one of them

Fake login pages, "accidental reports", diamond generators and pre-orders that don't exist: the tricks change clothes but follow the same script. Here is how to spot each one.

By Aloha TeamNovember 24, 2026, 14:11

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

In 2025 alone, Kaspersky blocked more than 2 million phishing attempts impersonating gaming platforms such as Steam, PlayStation and Xbox. The same year, it counted more than 20 million attempted infections by malicious programs disguised as gaming software, 18.5 million of them using Discord as bait. Gamers became a favorite target for a simple reason: a gaming account holds a balance, items and a saved card.

The good news is that almost every scam follows the same script. Once you know the six most common ones, you become much harder to catch.

1. The login page that looks like Steam's

A message arrives with "free skin", "vote for my team in the tournament" or a link to an item-betting site. Click it and a window identical to Steam's sign-in appears. It is a copy, and whoever types a username and password hands over the account. The rule is simple: only sign in by typing the official address or through the app, never via a link you received.

2. The "accidental report"

A stranger says they reported your account by mistake and it will be banned, but a "Steam agent" can fix it. The contact sends you to a Discord profile posing as support that asks for your password, your Steam Guard code or for you to "transfer your items to keep them safe". Steam Support does not contact users by chat or Discord. Any "Valve employee" who appears out of nowhere is a scammer.

3. Diamond, Robux or V-Bucks generators

Sites and videos promise 999,999 diamonds in Free Fire or unlimited Robux "just with your ID". That doesn't exist. In-game currency is only sold inside the games or through channels the companies authorize. The fake site usually asks for your game login, wants you to install an app or charges a fee "to release the balance". It is the scam that hits children the most, which is why it is worth talking about at home.

4. The gift card demanded in a hurry

Someone threatens, invents a debt or pretends to be an acquaintance and demands that the victim buy gift cards at a store, scratch off the code and send a photo. Within minutes the balance is spent or resold, and the trail is gone. The scam became so common that in June 2026 Valve decided to stop restocking physical Steam cards at retailers. Digital cards are still sold on the platform itself. No legitimate bill is paid with a gift card code.

5. The pre-order that doesn't exist

Every big release brings fake pre-order sites, files promising "early download" and impossible deals. GTA 6 was no exception: Kaspersky researchers found fake pages in Brazil that led victims to a bogus payment screen. For what is official about the game, see our GTA 6 guide. The safe purchase is through the console's store or well-known retailers.

6. The cheat or mod that comes with a virus

Programs promising cheats, unlocks or "optimizers" downloaded from random links are the shortest route to a password stealer. Many circulate on Discord servers, which explains Kaspersky's number. If it doesn't come from the official store or a well-known repository, don't install it.

The basic protection kit

Turn on two-step verification on every gaming account: on Steam, that means Steam Guard through the mobile app. Use different passwords for your email and for each platform. Keep recovery codes somewhere safe. And distrust any urgency: "do it now or lose your account" is a scammer's favorite line.

If the worst happens, go only to the platform's official support through its website or app. On Steam, after you regain access, check that the intruder didn't leave an API key registered, a trick used to keep watching your item trades.
