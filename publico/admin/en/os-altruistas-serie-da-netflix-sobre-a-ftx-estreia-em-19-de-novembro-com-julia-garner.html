The Altruists, Netflix's series on the fall of FTX, premieres on November 19

Julia Garner and Anthony Boyle play Caroline Ellison and Sam Bankman-Fried in eight episodes created by Graham Moore, with the Obamas as executive producers.

By Aloha TeamNovember 18, 2026, 19:12

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

Netflix premieres The Altruists on November 19, 2026, a limited series about the rise and fall of the cryptocurrency exchange FTX. Julia Garner plays Caroline Ellison and Anthony Boyle plays Sam Bankman-Fried, the two young people who, according to the story the series tells, tried to remake the financial system and ended up accused of stealing 8 billion dollars from customers.

What is known about the series

There are eight episodes, all exclusive to the platform, according to What's on Netflix and TV Insider. The creator, writer and showrunner is Graham Moore, an Oscar winner for The Imitation Game, and the co-showrunner is Jacqueline Hoyt. Barack and Michelle Obama are executive producers through Higher Ground Productions. Netflix describes the central pair as "Gen Z's own Bonnie & Clyde", and TV Insider says the plot is inspired by reporting in New York magazine.

A difference in dates deserves a note. TV Insider still listed the series as "TBA", while What's on Netflix and Showmetech's release list give November 19. Since Netflix has already released an official trailer and the date on its most recent pages, the 19th is the one that stands.

Cast

Besides Garner and Boyle, Alex Lawther appears as Sam Trabucco, Karan Soni as Nishad Singh, Naomi Okada as Claire Watanabe, Madison Hu as Constance Wang, Matt Rife as Ryan Salame and Eugene Young as Gary Wang. In supporting roles are Paul Reiser, Robin Weigert and Jennifer Grey. TV Insider also lists Hudson Williams in a recurring role. Julia Garner returns to Netflix, where she became known for the series Ozark, according to the headline of the What's on Netflix report.

What the series shows, and what it is not

According to the official synopsis, the two are idealistic young people who wanted to reshape the global financial system, until they were accused of stealing 8 billion dollars in customer funds. It is a drama based on real events, and that calls for care: a series dramatizes scenes, dialogue and motives that no viewer can check. Anyone who wants to separate fiction from what was established should look to journalistic coverage of the case, not the series, as the source of the facts.

Why it draws attention

There are two reasons. The first is the cast: Garner and Boyle arrive as leads of a limited series with an award-winning showrunner. The second is the subject, which remains familiar to the public years after the collapse of FTX, and which Netflix sells as a story of young people and money. The series is not a documentary, and the company itself classifies it as a limited crime drama.

How the series got here

The main cast was announced in May 2025, going by the date in the address of a Hypebeast report, and filming was set to begin in July 2025, according to the address of another, from What's on Netflix. These are details taken from titles and links, not from reading the articles in full, so treat them as an indication. What the sources confirm is that production is already finished, that the trailer is out and that the global premiere is set for November 19.

How to watch

The premiere is global and includes all episodes on Netflix, according to What's on Netflix. The video below is the official trailer, published on Netflix's channel, in English. Whether the series will please or not can only be known after the premiere, and this text will be updated with confirmed information, not trailer impressions.
