Why Brazilian productions are reaching the top of streaming charts worldwide

Senna, Caramelo and Radioactive Emergency topped Netflix's global chart for non-English titles. Here's how that ranking works and what these hits have in common.

By Aloha TeamNovember 27, 2026, 18:54

Radioactive Emergency, official trailer (Netflix Brasil, in Portuguese)
ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

In March 2026, a miniseries about the 1987 cesium-137 accident in Goiânia became Netflix's most-watched non-English series in the world. It wasn't an isolated case. Over the past two years, Brazilian productions have reached first place in that ranking several times, with stories as different as the biography of a Formula 1 driver and a drama about a mutt. Understanding how the ranking works helps show the real size of these results.

How Netflix's ranking works

Every week, Netflix publishes lists of the most-watched titles in the world, split into four categories: English-language films and series, and films and series in other languages. The main measure is views, calculated by dividing total hours watched by the title's runtime. That way, a short miniseries and a long film can be compared. The company also reports how many countries each title reached the local Top 10 in.

This means that leading the non-English list isn't the same as being the platform's most-watched title, since English-language productions usually post bigger numbers. Even so, that category includes Korean, Spanish, Japanese and dozens of other countries' series, and reaching the top of it is a significant achievement.

The cases that paved the way

Pedaço de Mim, a melodrama starring Juliana Paes and Vladimir Brichta, premiered in July 2024 and spent two straight weeks in first place among non-English series. In November of the same year, Senna, about the life of Ayrton Senna, also reached the top: according to Netflix, it logged more than 53 million hours watched in its first ten days and reached the Top 10 in 58 countries.

In October 2025, it was a film's turn. Caramelo, with Rafael Vitti and the dog that gives the movie its name, reached the Top 10 in 90 countries and passed 50 million views, becoming the platform's most successful Brazilian title worldwide. According to the company's engagement report, Brazilian productions grew 60% in global views in the second half of 2025.

Radioactive Emergency and the interest in true stories

The miniseries Radioactive Emergency, released on March 18, 2026, recreates the Goiânia radiological accident through the work of doctors and scientists. With five episodes, it logged more than 10.8 million views in its second week and reached the Top 10 in 55 countries, according to Netflix.

The case reinforces a trend. Series based on real events, such as DNA do Crime, which follows Federal Police investigations, also often appear among the most-watched in countries such as Argentina, Paraguay, Portugal and Nigeria.

What these hits have in common

Some factors repeat. The first is dubbing and subtitles in dozens of languages, which put a Brazilian series in the same catalog as an American one, on the same day, almost everywhere in the world. The second is genre: melodrama, sports drama, animal stories and true cases have an appeal that doesn't depend on knowing Brazil. The third is the short format. Miniseries of five or six episodes are easy to binge over a weekend, which speeds up their entry into the weekly lists.

There is also a showcase effect. When a Brazilian title reaches the top, the algorithm starts recommending it to more people in other countries, and foreign viewers who liked it often look for other Brazilian productions.
