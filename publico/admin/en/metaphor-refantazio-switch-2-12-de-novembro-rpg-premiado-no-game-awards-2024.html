Metaphor: ReFantazio comes to Switch 2 on November 12: the RPG that won three awards at The Game Awards 2024

The game sold a million copies on day one in 2024. The Switch 2 version looks like a straight port, and the Brazilian price and Portuguese subtitles still need checking.

By Aloha TeamNovember 7, 2026, 19:08

ShareWhatsAppXFacebookTelegram

One million copies in a single day: that is what Metaphor: ReFantazio sold when it launched on October 11, 2024, making it the fastest-selling game in Atlus history, according to RPG Site, Einerd, GameHall and Wikipedia. A little over two years later, the RPG arrives on Nintendo Switch 2 on November 12, 2026. The date appears in all six sources I read, and the announcement was made during the June 9 Nintendo Direct, according to four of them.

What the game is about

The story is set in the United Kingdom of Euchronia, a medieval fantasy world, according to Wikipedia. The kingdom is on the brink of ruin, and the fate of the throne is decided in a royal tournament. The protagonist, again according to Wikipedia, is an orphan of the Elda tribe and a childhood friend of the prince. He fights with powers called Archetypes, something like the Personas of the Persona series or a class system.

The game also organizes time like a calendar. Nintendo Life's summary puts it this way: by day, quests, dungeons and treasure hunting; by night, relationships and virtues. Wikipedia adds a rule that matters when planning ahead: missing the deadline for an important story event ends in a game over.

Who is behind it? The game comes from Studio Zero, part of Atlus. Einerd describes it as the same team as Persona. Wikipedia is more precise: it says this is a new studio, directed by Katsura Hashino, with artist Shigenori Soejima and composer Shoji Meguro, two names tied to Persona. The two descriptions do not contradict each other, but Wikipedia's is more detailed.

What the reviews said

Metacritic shows a score of 94, a figure that appears in three of the sources. Wikipedia specifies that it refers to the PS5 version, and that the PC and Xbox versions scored 92. At The Game Awards 2024, the game won in three categories: Best RPG, Best Narrative and Best Art Direction, as stated in four of the texts. Wikipedia also lists the RPG of the Year award at the D.I.C.E. Awards and the grand prize at the 2025 Japan Game Awards, and says the game passed 2 million copies, citing a SEGA presentation. These last details come from a single source.

What changes on Switch 2

From what is known, not much. Nintendo Life and Einerd say, cautiously, that it looks like a straight port with no exclusive content. None of the six pages gives resolution, frame rate or performance, and none says whether the extra content from the original release, such as the 2024 DLC, is included. Universo Nintendo lists compatibility with the console's touch screen.

As for the physical edition, four sources mention a limited-time SteelBook version at launch. Only Universo Nintendo gives the format: a game-key card, meaning the card acts as a key and the game is downloaded. Since this comes from a single source, check the packaging before you buy.

Price, and whether it comes in Portuguese

Nintendo Life lists US$39.99 in the United States and £34.99 in Europe and the United Kingdom. Universo Nintendo shows R$167.90 on the Brazilian Nintendo Store without saying whether this is the launch price in Brazil, and the site is the only one to give that figure. The same page tags the game with a "Em Português" (in Portuguese) label, but no source describes whether there are subtitles, menus or voice acting in the language. That is the kind of information the game's page on the Brazilian eShop answers better than any news report.

What is still unknown

Beyond performance and included content, nobody states the download size clearly, and the sources even disagree on who is listed as publisher: Nintendo Life and Universo Nintendo say SEGA, RPG Site says Atlus. The safest approach is to check the game's store page on launch day.

Sources: RPG Site, Nintendo Life, GameHall (in Portuguese), Einerd (in Portuguese), Universo Nintendo (in Portuguese) and Wikipedia.
